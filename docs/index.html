4. Experiment setup

The pipeline is fixed: prompts.csv to model answers, then results/scored_answers.csv, then this report. Only rows with status ok and non-empty answer text are scored.

Mention, rank, and recommendation are all decided by exact text checks on the answer, so they are repeatable and do not depend on a model's opinion. A recommendation is counted only when the answer puts Firebolt forward as a fit for the workload, not when it merely names it. The judge model writes only the explanation and factual-risk notes.

One prompt, P007, asks when not to use Firebolt. A good answer here explains where Firebolt does not fit, so recommending Firebolt is not the goal. P007 still counts toward the mention rate, but it is left out of the recommendation rate so a correct 'do not use it here' answer is not scored as a miss.

The visibility score is V = 2M + 3R + K + E - P. The formula is used for comparison within this run. It is not a market-share score or a product score.

Choosing between Firebolt and ClickHouse is a classic "managed service vs. open-source powerhouse" trade-off. ClickHouse: The "Control Everything" Engine ClickHouse is the industry standard for high-performance, real-time analytics. It is designed to abstract away the infrastructure so you can focus on SQL. * The Strengths: * Decoupled Storage and Compute: This is Firebolt's "killer feature." You can scale your compute clusters up or down independently of your storage. This is excellent for workloads with unpredictable spikes. * Developer Experience: It feels more like a traditional data warehouse (like Snowflake) but with the performance of an OLAP engine. It handles indexing (sparse indexes) and data distribution more automatically than ClickHouse. * Reduced Ops: You don't manage nodes, shards, or replication. At massive scale, this can become more expensive than a well-tuned, self-hosted ClickHouse cluster. --- The Decision Matrix Choose ClickHouse if: * You have a dedicated SRE/Platform team: You have the capacity to manage the infrastructure or are willing to pay for a managed service like ClickHouse Cloud. * You need extreme performance tuning: You have complex, custom data structures and need to squeeze every millisecond out of your queries. * You want portability: You need to run the same stack on-prem, in a private cloud, or across multiple public clouds. * You have a massive community safety net: You prefer a tool where you can find answers to almost any edge-case error on GitHub or StackOverflow. Choose Firebolt if: * You are a small team with limited DevOps bandwidth: You want to write SQL and get high performance without worrying about cluster topology or sharding. * Your workload is highly elastic: You have massive bursts of analytical traffic and want to spin up/down compute resources instantly without manual intervention. * You are already "all-in" on cloud-native: You are comfortable with a managed SaaS model and prioritize developer velocity over total control of the underlying engine. * You want a "Snowflake-like" experience for OLAP: You want the ease of use of a modern data warehouse but need the sub-second latency that Snowflake often struggles to provide for high-concurrency dashboards.
